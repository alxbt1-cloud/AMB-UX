Event Promotions Bottom Sheet

Role Lead UX Designer
Timeline 3 months
Team 2 Designers, 4 Developers, 1 Product Manager
Mobile Design Information Architecture Conversion Optimization Personalization UX research

We designed a dynamic bottom sheet component for event pages that intelligently surfaces relevant promotional offers, helping users discover and apply bonuses they're eligible for while placing bets on specific sporting events.

The Challenge

The betting platform offered numerous promotional offers, but users struggled to find and apply relevant ones:

  • Promotions were buried in a separate section away from betting flow
  • Users didn't know which offers applied to specific events
  • Complex terms and conditions created confusion and distrust
  • Low promotional uptake despite high marketing spend
  • Users frequently missed out on bonuses they were eligible for
  • No context-aware promotion discovery mechanism
entry point to promotions hidden in account menu
Promotional cards are lack context and are difficult to scan
The betslip is the first time customers are told about multis and often have to double back to forefil promotion's requirements

Research & Discovery

I conducted user interviews with 28 bettors about their promotional usage patterns, analyzed behavioral data from 100K+ user sessions, and reviewed support tickets related to promotions. I also performed usability testing on competitor apps to understand best practices for promotional discovery. Key insight: users wanted promotions presented at the right moment, not just on the home screen, and not hidden in menus

6 of 28
Of interviewed bettors mentioned lossing track of the promotion.
8 of 28
Of interviewed bettors mentioned being unclear what offers were available.
25 of 28
Of interviewed bettors said they would prefer promotions were visible on the event.

Solution

Designed an intelligent bottom sheet that appears on event pages, showcasing relevant promotions:

  • Context-aware filtering showing only promotions applicable to the current event
  • Clear visual hierarchy with promotion value prominently displayed
  • Expandable cards revealing full terms and conditions
  • One-tap application directly from the bottom sheet
  • Visual indicators showing which promotions user is already using
  • Smart sorting prioritizing highest-value and expiring-soon offers
  • Subtle entry animation triggered by scrolling behavior
  • Quick dismiss and easy re-access without disrupting betting flow

Results

The promotional bottom sheet significantly improved user engagement and conversion:

Key Learnings

This project reinforced the importance of contextual information delivery. By bringing promotions to users at the moment of decision-making rather than requiring them to search, we dramatically increased engagement. The bottom sheet pattern proved ideal for optional, supplementary information that enhances but doesn't interrupt the primary user journey.

Merchandise Promotional Offers

Role UX Designer & Strategist
Timeline 6 months
Stakeholders Product, Marketing, Merchandising
UX Strategy A/B Testing Cross-customer Design Revenue Optimization Statistics

Strategically integrated merchandise promotional offers throughout key betting user journeys to drive cross-category engagement while maintaining focus on the primary betting experience. The goal was to increase merchandise revenue without disrupting the betting flow.

The Challenge

The company wanted to promote their merchandise (team jerseys, betting-related apparel, fan gear) to betting users, but faced several obstacles:

  • Merchandise promotions felt disconnected from betting experience
  • Users perceived promotional content as intrusive or spammy
  • Risk of distracting users from their primary goal (placing bets)
  • Finding the right balance between promotion and user experience
  • Low awareness of merchandise offerings among betting customers
  • Need to respect different user mindsets throughout their journey
entry point to promotions hidden in account menu
Promotional cards are lack context and are difficult to scan
The betslip is the first time customers are told about multis and often have to double back to forefil promotion's requirements

Research & Discovery

I conducted journey mapping workshops with 20 users to understand their betting flow mindsets, analyzed clickstream data across 200K+ user sessions, and ran preference tests with 300 customers on different promotional approaches. I also interviewed merchandising team to understand inventory and margin considerations. Research revealed distinct "high-focus" (pre-bet) vs "low-focus" (post-bet, browsing) moments in user journeys.

Of interviewed bettors mentioned lossing track of the promotion.
8 of 28
Of interviewed bettors mentioned being unclear what offers were available.
25 of 28

Design Solution

Created a multi-touchpoint promotional strategy that respects user context:

  • Post-bet confirmation screen featuring team merchandise related to the bet placed
  • Subtle sidebar promotions on browse/discovery pages (low-focus moments)
  • Personalized merchandise recommendations in account dashboard
  • Event page banners for major games featuring limited-edition merchandise
  • Empty betslip state showcasing trending merchandise
  • Email integration after big wins suggesting celebratory merchandise
  • Zero promotions during active bet construction (high-focus moments)
  • Consistent visual language connecting promotions to betting brand

Results

Less customer contacts regarding missing or lost promotions and greater value perception:

The bottom sheet pattern proved ideal for optional, supplementary information that enhances but doesn't interrupt the primary user journey.

Customer Information Update Flow

Role Senior UX Designer
Timeline 5 months
Team 2 Designers, 5 Developers, Compliance Team, Legal
Form Design Compliance UX User Research KYC/AML
📋

Overview

Redesigned the customer information update and correction flow to streamline regulatory compliance (KYC/AML) while significantly improving user experience. This critical flow affects account verification, withdrawal processing, and regulatory compliance.

The Problem

The existing information update process was a major source of user frustration and compliance issues:

  • Complex, confusing form spanning multiple pages
  • Users didn't understand why certain information was required
  • High abandonment rate (73%) on information update flows
  • Unclear error messages leading to repeated submission failures
  • Document upload requirements were poorly explained
  • Long processing times with no status visibility
  • Resulted in delayed withdrawals and account restrictions
  • Spike in customer support contacts about verification

Research Process

Conducted 22 user interviews including sessions with users who had abandoned the flow, performed task analysis with 15 participants using think-aloud protocol, analyzed 500+ support transcripts about verification issues, and collaborated closely with compliance team to understand regulatory requirements. Also benchmarked verification flows from banking and fintech apps for best practices.

🔒
📄

Design Solution

Created a transparent, user-friendly verification experience:

  • Progressive disclosure breaking flow into logical, manageable steps
  • Clear progress indicator showing exactly where users are in the process
  • Plain-language explanations for why each piece of information is needed
  • Inline validation with helpful, specific error messages
  • Smart form that pre-fills existing verified information
  • Visual document upload guide with examples of acceptable documents
  • Real-time processing status dashboard
  • Proactive notifications about verification progress
  • Alternative verification paths for edge cases
  • Mobile-optimized camera integration for document capture
📱
✓
🎯

Impact

68%
Reduction in completion time
-81%
Decrease in abandonment rate
-64%

Lessons Learned

This project demonstrated that even mandatory regulatory processes can be user-friendly. The key was transparency — explaining the "why" behind requirements transformed user perception from annoyed compliance to understanding. Working closely with legal and compliance teams early in the design process was crucial to finding solutions that satisfied both regulatory requirements and user needs. The improved flow not only enhanced user experience but also reduced fraud and improved compliance rates.
